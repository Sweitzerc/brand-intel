1. Purpose and scope

The pipeline answers three kinds of question about goods arriving by sea at US Pacific ports (Hawaii and the West Coast, with Guam as AIS-only context):

  1. What arrives, from where, and how. Value and weight by port of unlading, HS6 product line, country of origin and month, split by vessel, container and air.
  2. How concentrated and exposed the flows are. Supplier-country concentration per port, port concentration per product, global exporter concentration, port activity and congestion from ship positions, and a clearly labelled model of how much rides on individual ships.
  3. Where the patterns look wrong. A library of red-flag checks drawn from published typologies (FATF, OFAC, US Department of Labor and others), each one documented with its sources and its innocent explanations.

Commodities in scope

GroupHS codesExamples
Semiconductors and cable8541, 8542, 8543, 8544Diodes, transistors, LEDs and PV cells; integrated circuits; electrical machines n.e.s.; insulated wire and cable
Critical minerals2609–2621, 2846Tin, chromium, tungsten, molybdenum, titanium, niobium/tantalum ores; metal-bearing slags and residues; rare-earth compounds

Ports in scope

Census ports 3201 Honolulu (including Barbers Point, assumed), 3202 Hilo, 3203 Kahului, 3204 Nawiliwili–Port Allen, 3206 Kona, 2704 Los Angeles, 2709 Long Beach, 2809 San Francisco, 2811 Oakland, 3001 Seattle and 3002 Tacoma. Apra Harbor, Guam, appears in AIS only, because Guam is outside the Census customs territory.

The central limitation No source in scope links a specific ship to a specific cargo. Census reports port × product × origin × month; AIS reports ship positions. The sources are joined at port × month and origin country × HS6 × period only. Anything said at ship level is an allocation model, and every red flag is a lead for review, never a finding.

Out of scope

  • Bills of lading, manifests or any other shipment-level commercial data (CBP manifest data would close the ship-to-cargo gap, but it is not used).
  • Profiling private individuals. The entities analysed are trade flows, trade lanes, countries and vessels; company names enter only through the analyst-supplied screen command.
  • Satellite AIS. US coastal receivers only, so ocean legs are invisible.

2. Architecture

The system is a batch pipeline driven by a single command-line program. Each ingest command writes to a local data directory, every API response is cached on disk, and one build step turns whatever inputs are present into tables, a Markdown report and a self-contained HTML dashboard. Steps whose inputs are missing are skipped rather than failing the build.

Design principles

  • Re-runnable and cheap to repeat. Processed AIS days are skipped; Census and Comtrade responses are cached under data/cache keyed by query without the API key, so re-runs cost no quota.
  • Degrade, don't fail. Missing inputs skip the dependent tables and checks, and the run log says which and why.
  • Observed vs modelled is explicit. Modelled outputs carry an est_ prefix and estimated=True; the dashboard puts them under a "MODELLED, not observed" banner.
  • Every judgement is documented next to the code that makes it. Scope constants live in config.py; each red-flag check carries its question, method, sources and innocent explanations.

Schema handling

AIS arrives in two layouts: the 2025+ snake_case schema (longitude before latitude, sentinel values already null) and the legacy CamelCase schema. Both are normalised to the 2025 layout; legacy sentinels (heading 511, speed 102.3, course 360, zero dimensions) become null, and the IMO prefix is stripped. Census responses are JSON arrays with a header row; predicate variables echoed back are de-duplicated and numeric fields coerced.

Grain of the fused fact table

6. Analytics

Vessel identity resolution

IMO is missing on about 65% of raw AIS rows. The registry takes, per MMSI, the most common non-null IMO, name, call sign and type over the whole period, and the median dimensions. The stable vessel key is IMO####### where known, otherwise MMSI#########. MMSIs broadcasting more than one IMO are flagged.

Port-call detection

Each point is assigned to the nearest port whose radius contains it. A visit is a run of points in one geofence, split when the ship leaves or goes silent for more than 6 h. A visit counts as a call when time at ≤ 0.5 kn reaches 2 h. Calls touching the edge of the date range are flagged as censored.

Anchorage wait

Slow time (≤ 0.5 kn) spent within 25 km of the port but outside the berth geofence, in the 72 h before the call's arrival, attached to the next call by the same ship at the same port. This is the only route-deviation signal coastal-only AIS supports.

Concentration metrics

Herfindahl–Hirschman Index on the 0–10,000 scale (over 2,500 = highly concentrated): supplier countries per port × HS6, ports per HS6, and world exporters per HS6 (Comtrade). Each comes with member count, top member and its share.

Mode split

Vessel, air and containerised shares of general import value per port × HS6, plus US dollars per kg by vessel. This answers the recon's second risk first: if chips mostly fly, the AIS side of the story applies mainly to cable and minerals.

Ship-level allocation (modelled)

MODELLED, not observed For each port × month × HS6, Census vessel weight and value are spread across the dry-cargo calls (AIS types 70–79) arriving that month, in proportion to a size proxy. Tankers are excluded. Missing dimensions are filled with the port-month median.
capacity: weight_i ∝ length × width × arrival_draft draft_drop: weight_i ∝ length × width × max(arrival_draft − departure_draft, 0) (calls with no usable draft change: capacity × port-month median drop ratio)

Outputs: per-call edges (vessel × port × month × HS6 with estimated kg and USD), single-ship dependence per port × month × HS6 (vessel HHI, largest ship and its share), per-ship exposure ranked by estimated value, and the port-month-HS6 cells that had tonnage but no detected call (left unallocated rather than forced onto a ship).

7. Red-flag framework

A registry of documented checks, each emitting rows in one shared schema, then rolled up per entity. Adding a check means writing one decorated function and a test; it appears in the report, the dashboard and the check library automatically.

Severity bands: high ≥ 0.75, medium ≥ 0.45, low below. Sources cited by the checks include the FATF TBML report (2006) and FATF/Egmont risk indicators (2021), Global Financial Integrity's mirror-trade method, FinCEN advisories, the OFAC/State/US Coast Guard Sanctions Advisory for the Maritime Industry (May 2020), the DOL List of Goods Produced by Child Labor or Forced Labor, the State Department TIP report, FATF jurisdiction lists and the Basel AML Index.

Entity roll-up

risk(entity) = 1 − Π over checks c of (1 − max score of c on that entity)

Several weak, independent signals outrank one weak signal but never reach certainty, and a check that fires every month counts once. Entities are trade flows (port ← origin · HS6), trade lanes (origin · HS6), origin countries and vessels.

Dashboard

One self-contained HTML file: no external scripts, fonts or network calls, so it opens offline and can be emailed or hosted as a static page. The build embeds the tables the page needs as JSON (with </ escaped), and the browser re-aggregates them as filters change.

Every chart has a table view and keyboard-reachable tooltips; the page supports light and dark themes and phone-width screens. The categorical palette was checked with a colour-vision-deficiency validator. Because the file embeds its data, sharing it shares the numbers.

The reference tables ship inside the package. PACIFIC_TRADE_CROSSWALKS points the pipeline at an edited copy without touching the installed files.

Licensing and terms

  • NOAA AIS, Census, World Port Index, OFAC SDN and the US CSL are US government data with no access constraints.
  • UN Comtrade free keys allow 500 calls a day; bulk downloads are a paid tier.
  • OpenSanctions is free for non-commercial use only.
  • Sources such as Equasis restrict automated collection and are deliberately not scraped.

Volume and run time

ItemEstimate
AIS download~200–300 MB per day; ~20 GB per quarter; ~80 GB per year compressed

Recommended first live run: all trade data and lists for one quarter, plus about one week of AIS, then scale AIS up once the outputs are checked.

Rate limits and resilience

  • HTTP retries with exponential backoff on 429 and 5xx; downloads are written to a temporary file and renamed on completion.
  • Comtrade calls are throttled to one per 1.1 s and capped at the daily budget per process.
  • A failed AIS day does not stop the others; re-running fetches only what is missing.

11. Limitations and guardrails

Known limitations

LimitationEffect and mitigation
No ship ↔ cargo linkShip-level figures are modelled and labelled; unallocated tonnage is reported, not forced onto ships
Coastal-only AISNo ocean legs, no ship-to-ship transfers at sea; Hawaii coverage is thin (16 cargo/tanker IMOs on the probe day)
Semiconductors may mostly flyThe mode split is shown first; AIS conclusions apply mainly to cable and minerals if the vessel share is low
Origin, not lading countryCensus CTY_CODE is where goods were made; transshipment hubs are invisible in Census and must be inferred
Disclosure suppressionSmall ports and narrow HS6 lines lose or move values; missing is not zero
HS6 breadthPrice checks can flag legitimate product-mix differences; HS10 detail (--hs10) is the follow-up
Crew-entered AIS static dataIdentity flags can be typos; corroborate with a ship registry
GuamAIS only; no Census trade data

Guardrails

A flag is a lead, not a finding Output language is "matches typology X, with this evidence and these caveats". Credible leads go to bodies that can act: FinCEN, CBP e-Allegations, the DEA tip line, or the National Human Trafficking Hotline.
  • Organisations and vessels, not private individuals. The pipeline builds no profiles of people.
  • No accusations in public. Naming a company or person as a launderer from public-data anomalies risks defamation; corroborate and route through the proper channels.
  • Data collection within the rules. APIs and bulk downloads only; no logins the analyst does not own; licence terms respected.
  • Demo output is marked. Synthetic runs carry a banner on the dashboard and the report, because they show real country, port and vessel-style names next to random numbers.
  • Human trafficking is covered through the forced-labour supply-chain angle only; victim-facing work (ads, social media) is left to specialist organisations.
